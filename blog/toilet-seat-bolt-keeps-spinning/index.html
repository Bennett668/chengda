A screw can turn without securing a toilet seat. The missing information is often what the screw is supposed to engage: a nut below the pan, a particular anchor, or another model-specific fixing. Continuing to turn it does not answer that question. This guide focuses on spinning and repeated loss of fixing after installation, with a documented Quick Attach example and a service card for requesting the correct parts.

01 BATHROOM KNOW-HOW

Name the symptom before ordering a screw

Record whether the screw rotates with no change in clamping, whether a previously secure base loosens again, or whether the seat lifts off a retained mounting base. Those descriptions point to different interfaces. A lid that falls quickly concerns the closing mechanism and should have a separate report. If the seat is unstable, keep it out of use while the fixing is assessed; do not sit on it to demonstrate the problem.

02 BATHROOM KNOW-HOW

Photograph the complete fixing, not just its head

Provide the seat model, pan model and the supplied kit or its parts sheet. Photograph the hinge base and any safely visible underside without loosening the assembly. A screw head does not reveal its thread, mating component or intended grip. Note when the fault began: first installation, after cleaning, or after the seat was removed. Do not prise off an unidentified cover to obtain a photograph.

03 BATHROOM KNOW-HOW

A documented example: Kohler Quick Attach

In Kohler’s Quick Attach instructions, the bolt passes down through the seat hinge. A wing nut approaches from below; only its conical top enters the bowl opening and catches the bolt thread. The specified final tightening is from above with a screwdriver, without holding that wing nut. This particular engagement sequence explains why knowing the whole kit matters.

This example is not a diagnosis of your spinning bolt or a fitting method for an expansion anchor. The interface diagram is an original reading aid, not a dimensioned parts drawing. Match the actual kit to its own current instructions before an installer changes anything.

04 BATHROOM KNOW-HOW

Give the supplier a useful fault card

Report which part moves relative to which stationary surface and whether the fault returns after the documented installation procedure. Ask the supplier to confirm the complete compatible kit, not only a replacement screw. If engagement never develops or parts appear damaged, stop further tightening. A larger screw, mixed nut, glue or improvised washer changes the arrangement without establishing compatibility.

05 BATHROOM KNOW-HOW

Verify the original complaint after repair

The installer should check the corrected assembly against the seat instructions, including required alignment, support and retention, before returning it to use. Record the approved kit identifier and what was replaced. Verify that the original spinning or loosening symptom is resolved separately from soft closing. A quietly closing lid does not confirm a secure fixing, and a photograph alone is not a load test.

Seat-fixing service card

Fill in observed facts; leave unconfirmed dimensions and causes blank.
  • Seat / pan / fixing-kit identifier
  • Parts-sheet or installation-instruction revision
  • Part that rotates / surface that stays still
  • Started at installation / after cleaning / after removal
  • Previous steps and whether loosening returned
  • Supplier-confirmed kit and installer’s verification

FAQ COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Does a spinning bolt mean the thread is stripped?

Not necessarily. The mating part may not be engaged or the kit may be unsuitable; its exact condition needs inspection.

Can I use the Quick Attach method on any seat?

No. It describes that named fixing system, not all toilet-seat anchors.
